# Raising Humans — Ethical Principles

**Status:** v0.1 draft — agreed philosophical direction, open to refinement

## Purpose and authority

These principles govern educational recommendations, activities, assessment practices, tools and governance. They establish boundaries that cannot be overridden merely for convenience, efficiency, measurable achievement or parental ambition.

The framework adopts a **capabilities-based understanding of flourishing**, explicitly recognizes **children's rights**, and treats **progressive autonomy as a central developmental aim**, balanced with relationships, protection and responsibility.

### EP-01 — Unconditional human dignity

Every child possesses inherent worth, independent of achievement, ability, behavior, compliance or perceived potential. Educational methods must not condition dignity or belonging on performance.

### EP-02 — Respect for individuality

Each child is a distinct person with an emerging identity, temperament, interests, abilities and aspirations. Education supports individual development rather than imposing a predetermined personality or life trajectory.

### EP-03 — Protection and care

Children depend on adults for protection, stability and support. Safety, fundamental needs and well-being take precedence over educational ambitions. Protection should be proportionate and evolve with developing capabilities.

### EP-04 — Relationships and belonging

Trust, affection, respect and secure relationships are central to development. Educational approaches must not systematically sacrifice relational well-being for compliance or performance.

### EP-05 — Progressive autonomy

Children should increasingly make decisions, exercise judgment, assume responsibilities and learn from consequences. Autonomy is a central aim, **not** isolation or independence at any cost. Readiness depends on capability, safety, circumstances and evolving capacities—not chronological age alone.

### EP-06 — Whole-person development

Intellectual, emotional, physical, relational, moral, creative and practical development deserve consideration. Academic or economic success must not dominate by default.

### EP-07 — Freedom of thought and conscience

Children should be able to question assumptions, explore ideas, develop beliefs and revise views. Parents may share convictions and traditions while respecting children's emerging independent thought and conscience.

### EP-08 — Compassion, reciprocity and responsibility

Agency exists alongside responsibilities toward others. Education should foster empathy, fairness, honesty, cooperation and consideration of consequences.

### EP-09 — Pluralism with ethical boundaries

Raising Humans respects legitimate cultural, philosophical, religious and familial differences. Pluralism does not imply neutrality toward harm, discrimination, coercion or violations of fundamental rights.

### EP-10 — Children's rights and participation

Children are rights-holders, not merely recipients of adult care. The framework is informed by international children's rights principles, including the **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child**: non-discrimination, best interests, survival and development, participation, and respect for evolving capacities.

Children's views should be heard and given appropriate weight in decisions affecting them. Rights protections are not contingent on demonstrated competence.

Reference: [UN Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-child).

### EP-11 — Intellectual honesty and humility

The framework distinguishes **normative commitments** (what we value), **empirical claims** (what evidence suggests) and **practical recommendations** (what we propose doing). Uncertainty, limitations and disagreement must be acknowledged.

### EP-12 — Proportionality and non-instrumentalization

Educational demands should be proportionate to anticipated benefits, costs, risks and effects on family life. Children must not be treated as instruments of parental ambition, economic productivity or societal objectives.

### EP-13 — Revisability and accountability

Principles and their applications remain open to reasoned criticism. Changes must document rationale, consequences and compatibility with the project's purpose. Fundamental ethical changes require more rigorous review than ordinary content or technical revisions.

## Applying the principles

Major educational proposals should be checked against these principles. Where values conflict in practice—for example protection versus autonomy—the tension and reasoning must be explicit. The framework should guide judgment, not pretend to eliminate it.

These principles describe the project's ethical commitments; they are not presented as empirical findings. Educational methods derived from them must be evaluated separately for evidence, effectiveness, suitability and risk.
