# Raising Humans — Vision

**Status:** v0.1 draft — agreed philosophical direction, open to refinement  
**Scope:** Universal, pluralist, parent-first

## 1. Purpose

Raising Humans exists to help parents nurture children into capable, compassionate, autonomous and fulfilled human beings.

Raising children is not primarily about producing academic achievement, professional success, social conformity or a predetermined personality. It is about helping each child develop the capabilities, relationships, judgment and freedom needed to live a meaningful life, contribute to the world and increasingly direct their own future.

## 2. Mission

Provide parents with an open, evidence-informed and practical framework for supporting whole-person development, from early childhood toward independent adulthood.

The framework should help parents understand different dimensions of development; create meaningful learning opportunities; support progressively greater responsibility and autonomy; build supportive relationships and environments; reflect on their own parenting practices; and adapt to each child's needs, interests and circumstances.

## 3. Human flourishing: capabilities and opportunities

Human flourishing is an ongoing process, not a fixed destination or measurable achievement. The project's primary concern is enabling people to develop **capabilities and genuine opportunities** to pursue different worthwhile lives—not requiring everyone to exhibit the same virtues, preferences or outcomes.

Relevant dimensions include:

- **Dignity:** Recognizing one's inherent worth and that of others.
- **Agency:** Making informed choices, accepting responsibility and shaping one's life.
- **Capabilities:** Learning, reasoning, creating, solving problems and navigating practical challenges.
- **Relationships:** Building meaningful connections characterized by care, respect and reciprocity.
- **Ethical judgment:** Considering consequences, obligations and the rights of others.
- **Well-being:** Caring for physical and psychological health while navigating difficulties.
- **Meaning:** Exploring identity, values, purpose and belonging.
- **Contribution:** Participating constructively in communities and, where appropriate, challenging and improving them.
- **Adaptability:** Continuing to learn and responding thoughtfully to uncertainty.

These dimensions are interconnected. No single dimension is a universal measure of success. Well-being and character matter, but neither a fixed virtue checklist nor a happiness score defines flourishing.

## 4. Children are people now

Children have dignity, interests, relationships and emerging agency in the present—not merely as future adults. Childhood has intrinsic value. Play, curiosity, affection, discovery, joy, rest and belonging are not simply instruments for later success.

Parents have responsibilities to protect and nurture children while recognizing their evolving capacities for participation and independent judgment.

## 5. Parents as guides and learners

Parents are caregivers, role models, guides and fellow learners, not perfect instructors or optimizers of every moment. Their relationships, decisions, habits and willingness to reflect shape the developmental environment.

Raising Humans should support parental judgment, not replace it with rigid instructions.

## 6. Universality, pluralism and rights

The framework aspires to address fundamental dimensions of human development across cultures and societies, while acknowledging diversity in family structures, beliefs, traditions and visions of a good life.

The core distinguishes ethical commitments, empirical claims and practical recommendations. Families may adapt approaches to their contexts without undermining children's dignity, safety or fundamental rights.

International children's rights principles, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, inform this commitment. In particular, children are rights-holders whose views merit consideration in accordance with their evolving capacities. Rights references are an ethical and legal orientation, not a claim that every jurisdiction implements them identically.

## 7. Preparing for an uncertain world

Children will inherit a world shaped by technological change, artificial intelligence, environmental challenges and evolving institutions. Rather than predict one ideal future, the framework emphasizes durable capabilities: judgment, adaptability, creativity, collaboration, practical competence, ethical reasoning and independent thought.

Education should prepare children both to participate effectively in society and to question and improve it.

## 8. Boundaries

Raising Humans is not a method for producing standardized adults, ranking children, replacing individualized professional care, prescribing one culture's good life or turning every family moment into a lesson.

## 9. Long-term aspiration

Success means parents are better equipped to create supportive developmental environments and children have greater opportunities to become self-directed adults who can care for themselves, relate well to others and contribute meaningfully.

The framework should help parents become less necessary as directors of their children's lives without becoming less important as people in those lives.
